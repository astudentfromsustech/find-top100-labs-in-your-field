Method

The roster comes from OpenAlex: every work matching the field query (2019–2026) is walked, and the people who led each paper are counted — first author, last author, and every corresponding author, co-corresponding included. Position alone is not enough: on this corpus 52 % of papers have more than one corresponding author and 42 % have one outside the first/last slots, so counting by position misses a real PI on nearly half the literature. Plain middle authorship is ignored, so a 900-author consortium paper cannot mint hundreds of phantom labs.

Secondary corresponding authors are counted fractionally: with k corresponding authors the primary one (last in the author list) earns 1.0 and each other earns 1/k. That choice matters less than it sounds — moving from "secondary corresponding authors count nothing" to 1/k shifts ranks by a median of 13 places, while moving from 1/k to full credit shifts them by 3.

Where the weights come from

There is no objective weighting, so each page ships the published methodologies of the major rankings as presets — click one and watch the order change:

Output only / Tool adoptionthe two crude extremes, for contrast

The honest gap: every one of those rankings spends 25–45 % on reputation surveys — asking academics who they rate — and no bibliometric method can reproduce that. Here it is redistributed onto citation excellence, with h-index standing in for career standing. GitHub stars carry 0 % by default because only some labs have a curated repo list; scoring them would rank "we catalogued this lab's tools" as "this lab's tools are used".

Each indicator is log-transformed, z-scored across the ranked labs and rescaled to 0–100 before weighting. PP(top 10 %) is reported only for labs with at least 8 led papers — the Leiden Ranking sets a 800-publication floor for the same reason: a rate over a tiny denominator is noise, and at a floor of 3 papers ten labs here tied at a meaningless 100 %.

What this is not

  • Not a judgement of scientific quality. It measures publication and citation footprint in one field's literature, which is a proxy at best.
  • Consortium papers inflate every co-author; large-consortium fields therefore reward participation.
  • Co-first authorship is invisible. "These authors contributed equally" lives in a paper's footnotes, not in its metadata — no bibliometric database carries it — so a co-first author who is not also first, last or corresponding earns nothing here. This under-counts junior authors in particular.
  • An author is a proxy for a lab. OpenAlex sometimes splits one person across records or merges two people into one; obvious cases are pinned by hand, the rest are not.
  • The field query defines the field: a different query gives a different roster. The corpus size behind each ranking is stated on its card above.
