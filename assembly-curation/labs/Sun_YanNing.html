A hard-to-measure state soft-sensing method for aircraft large-component robotic assembly systems based on continual transformercorresponding author

2026-01-22 · Digital Twin · 0 citations · doi:10.1080/27525783.2026.2617706

The robotic assembly system requires high-precision positioning to ensure assembly quality. However, dynamic disturbances such as joint thermal deformation, load fluctuations, and environmental variations continuously alter system states, causing deviations between predefined parameters and actual conditions, thereby degrading measurement and control accuracy. These challenges are exacerbated by conventional static deep learning (DL) models, which rely on offline training and struggle with distribution shifts and catastrophic forgetting in multi-condition or evolving environments. To address these issues, this study proposes an intelligent monitoring framework based on an incremental spatiotemporal soft-sensing transformer (IS³-Transformer), which integrates a spatiotemporal multi-head continual attention mechanism and a regularised elastic weight consolidation (Reg-EWC) algorithm for assembly quality prediction. In this framework, the soft-sensing task of estimating end-effector pose errors is formulated as spatiotemporal dependency modelling, where continual attention captures the robotic kinematic topology and the temporal evolution of process states. Reg-EWC introduces task-similarity-aware regularisation to preserve learned knowledge while accommodating new process data, mitigating catastrophic forgetting. Validated on real industrial datasets from aircraft large-component assembly scenarios, the framework demonstrates superior prediction accuracy and adaptive learning capability under multiple operating conditions, outperforming conventional deep learning approaches in practical industrial settings.

Online sequential decision making of multi-stage assembly process parameters based on deep reinforcement learning and its application in diesel engine productioncorresponding author

2025-09-02 · Journal of Manufacturing Systems · 9 citations · doi:10.1016/j.jmsy.2025.08.012

Maintaining fixed parameters during batch assembly of complex mechanical products often results in quality inconsistencies due to time-varying operational conditions, including equipment performance degradation, production environment disturbance, and operator skill variations. This operational reality necessitates online parameter adaptation mechanisms to counteract progressive quality deviations. While complex assemblies inherently involve sequential multi-stage workflows across distributed stations, conventional optimization strategies often employ monolithic parameter adjustments that neglect error propagation effects and inter-stage quality interdependencies. To address the dual challenges of dynamic operating conditions and multi-stage coordination, this study proposes an online sequential decision-making framework based on deep reinforcement learning. First, a causal inference model for assembly quality prognosis is constructed by integrating the greedy equivalence search algorithm with domain-specific expert knowledge, enabling systematic modeling of multi-stage quality dependencies. Subsequently, the multi-stage parameters optimization problem is formalized as a Markov decision process, with innovatively defined state space as assembly progress, action space as adjusted parameters range, and physics-informed reward function derived from quality inference results. Building on this, the proximal policy optimization algorithm is improved by stage-aware experience replay and gradient alignment constraints to learn the optimal policy, and then select the optimal action. Experiments on a real-world diesel engine assembly dataset demonstrate a 17.16 % improvement in product qualification probability, significantly outperforming conventional methods. The proposed framework effectively captures time-varying assembly characteristics and achieves cross-stage parameter coordination through sequential decision-making, offering a novel data-driven solution for quality control in complex product assembly systems.

A Multi-Class Imbalanced Learning Method Using Random Balanced Sampling for Predicting the Quality Level of Diesel Enginesco-author

2024-08-07 · preprint / no venue on record · 1 citation · doi:10.1109/icamal62577.2024.10919696

Numerous process items and procedures in the assembly process of a diesel engine have a direct and significant impact on its performance, and accurate and effective assembly process control is a key technical bottleneck in improving the quality consistency of batch-produced diesel engines. Accurate prediction of assembly quality can provide support for online control of assembly quality. However, due to the significant class imbalance between qualified and unqualified products of diesel engine assembly quality, the traditional imbalance learning makes it difficult to capture the small changes between classes. Therefore, an improved random balance sampling method is proposed, combined with the LightGBM classifier to create a quality prediction model. Firstly, class balancing of the training dataset is achieved by generating random weights for each class and fusing oversampling and undersampling. Then, a LightGBM-based quality prediction model is built. Finally, the effectiveness of this approach is validated using real industrial diesel engine assembly data. The comparison results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional resampling techniques. This study provides an accurate quality prediction model, laying the groundwork for the assembly quality control model of diesel engines.

Deviation Propagation Network for Key Quality Factors Identification of Complex Assembly Systemsco-author

2024-08-07 · preprint / no venue on record · 0 citations · doi:10.1109/icamal62577.2024.10919718

The assembly process of complex products involves many process links, which is a dynamic process with multiple variables and complex coupling. Effective quality control of the assembly process plays an important role in improving the consistency of complex product quality. However, in the actual assembly process, even if the quality of each component meets the design requirements, deviations still gradually accumulate and spread in the process, and it is ultimately difficult to ensure the assembly quality. To effectively identify the key assembly features and the deviation propagation paths in the assembly process, this paper investigates the assembly deviation propagation problem of complex assemblies based on complex network theory. Firstly, based on the measured data, feature surface information, and assembly process, a coupled network model of parts-features is constructed for describing the complex assembly systems by taking assembly parts as nodes and assembly relationships as edges. Then, the node importance indexes of the assembly deviation propagation network are measured to identify the key assembly features, and trace and control the corresponding deviation assembly nodes. Finally, as a typical complex multi-stage process, the diesel engine assembly system is used for validation. Through experimental comparison with actual assembly processes, the results show that this method can effectively model the deviation flow in multi-stage assembly processes, identify key quality factors, and achieve deviation traceability.
